## Q1. What is the correct sequence before an objective fight, and why does arriving first matter?

### Takeaway
Every source found gives the same order:
1. **About 90 s out:** shove the most important wave(s) and reset (귀환) with gold spent.
2. **About 60 s out:** put down vision, including a Control Ward in or next to the pit.
3. **About 30 s out:** arrive, sweep, and hold the space in front of the pit (자리 선점). Then decide whether to fight or trade (교환).

Arriving first matters because the late team has to walk through your vision and your lane priority. They have to face-check (얼굴로 부쉬 확인) or give up the objective. The early team gets to choose where the fight happens.

### Cited Findings
- **Boostroom's 2026 guide on setup:**
  - Setup "begins about 90 seconds before an objective".
  - "Dragon fights are won before they start."
  - Waves "decide who arrives first and whether you can safely start".
  - Push "the most important wave(s) about 90 seconds before the objective", then set vision and decide on a fight plan "about 30 seconds before".
  - — [Boostroom objective control guide](https://boostroom.com/blog/objective-control-guide-dragons-herald-baron-and-setup-basics) (search summary; page not fetchable)
- **Boostroom on why arriving first wins:**
  - "If you arrive first with waves pushed and vision placed, the enemy is forced to face-check."
  - Failure case: "no waves → you lose towers while doing objective, or you arrive late".
  - "If you arrive first, don't drift away."
  - Start the dragon "if the enemy is late or split".
  - — [Boostroom](https://boostroom.com/blog/objective-control-guide-dragons-herald-baron-and-setup-basics) (search summary)
- Clearing lanes before an objective "prevents the enemy applying pressure elsewhere during the contest". — [Guild Order objective timing summary](https://guildorder.com/games/hok/wiki/objective-timing-and-macro-play). This entry is from Guild Order's **Honor of Kings** wiki. Only the general principle carries over to LoL.
- Ward "both entrances and possible flank positions of the river at least 30 seconds before the objectives spawn". — [Pro Game Guides warding](https://progameguides.com/lol/how-to-get-better-at-warding-in-league-of-legends/) (search summary; not dated 2026)
- Mobatrainer's teamfighting guide (Patch 26.10/26.13): "sweep flank wards before any major objective fight". — [Mobatrainer teamfighting](https://mobatrainer.com/learn/teamfighting) (search summary)
- **Korean champion guides** (lolstats.gg 2026, repeated across many champion pages):
  - "사이드 운영보다는 본대에 합류하여 오브젝트 앞 자리를 먼저 잡으세요." (Join the main group and take the space in front of the objective first, rather than playing a side lane.)
  - "오브젝트 교전 30초 전에 주변 부쉬에 [와드]를 깔아 시야를 장악하고" (Ward the nearby brush 30 s before the objective fight to take vision control.)
  - Win pattern: catch a key enemy carry to make it 5v4, then fight ("상대 주요 딜러를 … 낚아채서 5:4 구도를 만든 뒤 싸우는 것").
  - — [lolstats.gg Nautilus guide](https://lolstats.gg/ko/champions/nautilus/guide); [lolstats.gg Blitzcrank guide](https://lolstats.gg/ko/champions/blitzcrank/guide). These are templated pages, so they show common wording more than expert analysis.
- **Earlier project notes** (sourced in phase_playbook.md Q4):
  - mid.gg sequence: Control Ward in the pit at 60 s, deep ward at 45 s, river-entrance ward at 30 s.
  - Common mistakes: burning Smite charges about 45 s before Dragon, recalling as it spawns, and dying with 1,200+ g unspent.
- **Korean framing from earlier notes** (korean_turn_concept.md):
  - The team that takes **선턴** (first turn) before the spawn controls the vision and the fight.
  - "나오기 90초 전 귀환, 60초 전 시야 작업, 30초 전 자리 선점과 라인 주도권 확인" (recall 90 s before spawn, vision work at 60 s, take position and confirm lane priority at 30 s) is the natural Korean phrasing.

### Inferences
**Why each step has a mechanical reason:**
- **Reset at 90 s.** A recall takes 8 s, plus the walk back. You need gold spent before the 30 s window. Arriving on the old item set loses the stat check, and a recall mid-setup gives up the arrival race.
- **Shove waves first.** Two reasons:
  - A crashed wave forces the enemy laner to stay and clear it, or to lose plates. The plates are permanent and plated on inner turrets in 2026.
  - Your laner is free to walk to the pit first.

  So wave state decides the head-count at the pit, and head-count decides the fight.
- **Vision at 60 s, not 30 s.** Wards placed at 30 s are placed while the enemy is already walking in. Placing them at 60 s lets you see them arrive, and it gives your sweepers time to clear their wards. Without sight of the enemy, your team cannot start the objective safely.
- **Arriving first** turns the enemy's approach into a chokepoint problem:
  - They walk through your wards and the narrow river or jungle entrances, so they show where they are coming from.
  - You stand in the open area in front of the pit, or in brush you have swept, and get the first engage, poke or pick.
  - This is the "face-check" mechanism Boostroom describes.
- **For a Platinum jungler**, the practical order is:
  1. Clear camps toward the objective side.
  2. Base at about 90 s with the support or mid.
  3. Ping lanes to shove.
  4. Walk in with the team, not alone. The jungler alone in the river at 30 s is the easiest pick.

## Q2. How do you decide to fight or trade at an objective, and how do you play when the enemy starts it?

### Takeaway
**Fight (한타 / 받아치기)** when the count of usable resources favors you:
- numbers
- HP and mana
- key ultimates
- Flash / Teleport
- item spikes
- you arrived first

**Trade instead (맞교환 / 반대편 챙기기)** when the enemy arrived first with numbers and vision. Take something of equal or higher value on the other side. Never give the objective for free.

**When the enemy starts the objective:**
- You get a timing advantage. They are spending HP and cooldowns on the monster.
- Go in at the moment the monster is low enough to threaten a steal, or from a flank in fog.
- Do not walk single-file through the chokepoint.

### Cited Findings
- **Mobatrainer (Patch 26.x):** force fights "when the enemy is missing a key player, a major ultimate, or Flash, or when you have a numbers advantage near an objective". — [Mobatrainer teamfighting](https://www.mobatrainer.com/guides/teamfighting) (search summary)
- **Boostroom:**
  - The core of setup is to "decide early whether you are contesting or trading".
  - Commit to an objective "when the enemy is split or their jungler is on the far side of the map".
  - Track the enemy jungler.
  - — [Boostroom](https://boostroom.com/blog/objective-control-guide-dragons-herald-baron-and-setup-basics) (search summary)
- **Trading principle:**
  - "never give it up for free. Always trade up by taking something of higher value".
  - "Create pressure on one side of the map to open up an objective on the other."
  - Common trade rule: "if enemy takes Baron, you take Dragon (or vice versa)".
  - — search summary of general objective guides and [Guild Order objectives reference](https://guildorder.com/ko/games/league/wiki/objectives-reference). Quality is mixed; the summary itself flagged some as machine-translated copies.
- **Enemy flanker:** "if the enemy who is trying to flank is low-threat, then you should be able to continue taking the objective". — [Mobalytics: How to ward while sieging](https://mobalytics.gg/lol/guides/how-to-ward-while-sieging) (search summary; updated Oct 30, 2025)
- **Enemy jungler location:** if the enemy jungler "is alive and nearby when you're attempting to take Baron, they have the opportunity to steal the objective". — [Mobalytics 5 tips to secure Baron](https://mobalytics.gg/lol/guides/5-tips-to-secure-the-baron-safely) (search summary; date unknown)
- **Don't fight right after Baron:** avoid fighting "right after taking Baron if the game is even, since you may lose the buff". — search summary of general guides ([Mobalytics Baron tips](https://mobalytics.gg/blog/4-tips-secure-baron-league-of-legends/)). Older material.
- **2026 rule that shapes contests** (from objectives_rules_2026.md, citing Riot 26.1):
  - Dragon Vengeance gives the team **behind** in drakes 15/30/45% damage reduction against the team ahead. Its bonus damage to champions was removed.
  - Drakes have up to 35% more effective HP, rising for the team near soul.
- **LCK 2026 example of counter-engaging on an objective:**
  - At a dragon fight, KT traded Syndra for Olaf, then "scored heavily by picking up three unanswered kills in the resulting 4-v-4 skirmish".
  - Trundle's pillar isolated a target on a retreat to set up the fight.
  - — [Inven Global: KT vs DK 1-0](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25370/kt-strike-first-against-dk-with-fantastic-aggro-juggling-1-0) (snippet; year not confirmed on page)
- **LCK example of a forced start being punished:**
  - Gen.G had Baron and a 3,000+ gold lead.
  - "KT was forced to start the Elder Dragon first, and Lee Sin stole it, which turned the game."
  - — [Inven Global: Two Elder Steals… Gen.G vs KT PO R2](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25450/two-elder-steals-and-a-stolen-victory-geng-takes-game-1-against-kt-rolster-in-po-r2) (snippet; year unverified)
- **Korean usage** (korean_terms.md): the trade is called **맞교환 / 반대편 챙기기 / "바텀 주고 탑 먹기"** (give bot, take top). '크로스맵' is rare in KR.

### Inferences
**Fight-or-trade checklist (inference; each line is a resource that changes the fight's expected value):**
1. **Numbers.** Count who is alive and who is within about 20 s of the pit. One missing enemy (5v4) is the strongest single signal; every source above lists it first. The reason is that damage and crowd control scale with bodies, and the monster soaks the enemy's DPS while they are fewer.
2. **Ultimates and summoners.** Enemy engage ult down, or enemy Flash or Teleport down, means you fight. Their engage ult down removes their only way to start the fight. Their Teleport down removes the flank arrival from the side lane.
3. **HP and mana.** If your team is below about 60% average HP before the monster even starts, you trade. Whoever starts the objective loses HP to the monster. Grubs and drakes deal 50% more damage to non-champions, but they still hit champions.
4. **Item spikes and the gold situation.** A completed item usually beats the other team's component items. This is the reason for the 90 s reset.
5. **Who arrived first and holds vision.** A late team should not walk into a lit chokepoint. It should either flank from fog or trade.
6. **Value of the objective:**
   - A soul-point drake or Elder: always contest, or at least threaten a steal.
   - The first drake, or grubs when you are behind: trade freely.

   This matches the Korean "첫 드래곤은 무리하지 않는다" (don't force the first dragon) / "4번째 용은 절대 내주지 않는다" (never concede the fourth dragon) advice in phase_playbook.md.

**When the enemy starts the objective (contest vs trade):**
- **Contest if:**
  - you have similar numbers,
  - your engage is available,
  - and you can reach a flank in fog.

  The best moment is when the monster is around half HP. They have committed, and their HP and cooldowns are partly spent. A dragon or Baron at low HP also threatens a Smite steal, which forces them to turn.
- **Trade if:**
  - they have numbers or vision,
  - and you have a crashed wave toward a turret or another objective on the other side.

  Take plates, a turret, grubs or Herald, or invade camps, and announce it early. A late "trade" ping after the fight has started usually means losing both the objective and the trade.
- **Dragon Vengeance (2026):** at 2–3 drakes down, the team behind takes 30–45% less damage from the team ahead. That makes it **more** reasonable for the team behind to fight at soul point than older guides suggest. Coaches and guides haven't stated this; it is a mechanical inference.

### Gaps
- No source gives numeric thresholds (HP %, gold lead, level gap) for fight vs trade. All thresholds above are inferences.
- No KR coach video transcript found on 교환 판단 (deciding when to trade).

## Q3. Smite: 2026 values, securing and stealing, positioning, and when the jungler should look to steal vs fight

### Takeaway
**2026 Smite values:**
- Base Smite: **600**. 26.1 changed the tiers from 600/900/1200 to **600/1000/1400**.
- The jungle Role Quest upgrades Smite. The aggregator below names the 1400 tier "Primal Smite".
- Smite does true damage.
- 26.12: Smite's recharge timer was cut from 1:30 to **1:20**.

**Who wins a Smite fight (강타 싸움):** both junglers usually have the same top-tier Smite. The winner is whoever counts the monster's HP correctly and is in range at the right moment.

**When to bait:** the team taking the objective should hold Smite until the last possible moment. Because Smite only works in range, the stealer's job is to get close, and the defender's job is to kill or knock away the enemy jungler first.

### Inferences
**Smite math (inference):**
- Smite threshold = Smite damage + the burst you can add within about 0.5 s. That burst includes:
  - an execute or true-damage ability (Nunu Q, Cho'Gath R, and similar),
  - your team's DPS during your reaction time.
- At 1400 true damage, call "Smite range" at roughly 1400 plus team DPS times your reaction delay.
  - With 4–5 champions hitting Baron, the monster can drop 1,000+ HP per second. So waiting for exactly 1400 is often too late, and the danger window is about 2000–2500 HP.
  - Train on the HP bar, not raw numbers.
  - The exact numbers depend on champion levels, because epic monsters scale with levels in 2026.

**Who smites, and team positioning (inference):**
- The defending jungler stands **inside the pit, next to the monster, between it and the side the enemy will come from**. That spot is in range, and it forces the enemy jungler to walk past your team to reach range.
- Teammates stand **at the entrance** (zoning; see Q5). Their job is to body-block and to save CC (crowd control: stun, knock-up, knockback, suppress) for the enemy jungler, not for the frontline.
  - The single highest-value CC in an objective fight is the one that stops the enemy jungler as they enter Smite range.
  - The reason is that Smite cannot be cast while stunned, knocked up or suppressed.

**Baiting (inference):**
- Show a fake Smite threat by standing in range. Or stop hitting to "pause" the monster above Smite range until the enemy jungler commits or uses Flash.
- Then resume hitting and Smite after their burst or Flash is spent.
- The stealer's bait is the reverse: walk visibly away, then return from fog.

**Steal vs fight for the jungler (inference):**
- Look to steal when:
  - your team cannot win the 5v5,
  - the enemy has already started,
  - and you have a mobility tool, Flash, or range (a long-range ability plus Smite).
- Otherwise, fight next to your team. A jungler who dives alone for a steal, and misses, gives the enemy a 5v4 plus the objective.
- If your Smite is on cooldown or lower tier, do not 50/50. Fight or trade instead.

## Q4. Specific 2026 objectives: how each changes the fight

### Takeaway
Each objective has its own fight geography:
- **Grubs (8:00):** three monsters that spawn voidmites. Smite can't win them alone.
- **Herald (15:00–19:45):** the same top pit. Lower value in 2026, so it is the cheapest objective to trade.
- **Dragon:** the pit and its surroundings change after the 2nd drake (Elemental Rift). Four drakes give soul. Dragon Vengeance (15/30/45%) changes who can contest.
- **Baron (20:00, 6:00 respawn):** one of three random forms. Two of them reshape the pit: a front wall, or a long side tunnel.
- **Elder (6:00 after soul):** an execute buff on any champion below 20% HP for 150 s. Elder fights decide games and are the most common steal stage.

  - Baron first kill 20:10
  - no Atakhan kills

## Q5. Zoning, "setting up the pit", chokepoints vs open areas, fighting in fog, and Korean community terms

### Takeaway
**Zoning (존 잡기 / 자리 잡기)** means standing your team at the pit entrances so the enemy has to walk through a narrow approach to reach the monster or Smite range.

**Setting up the pit (용 세팅 / 바론 세팅 / 사전 작업):**
1. Control Ward and sweep inside the pit.
2. Wards on the enemy's approach routes.
3. Your bodies on the chokepoints.

**Who should fight where:**
- Teams with AoE engage (Ornn, Amumu, Sejuani, Orianna-type combos) want the enemy in a chokepoint.
- Teams with range and poke want the open area in front of the pit, where they can kite.

**Fighting in fog (안개 속 한타):** use the side you have not been seen on. Engagers should wait in swept brush, and flankers should arrive late from a direction with no enemy wards.

### Inferences
**Chokepoint vs open area, and why:**
- AoE CC hits more targets when the enemy is bunched in a narrow river entrance or jungle path. So engage and zone teams should hold the entrance, and wait for the enemy to come through.
- Poke and kite teams should hold the open area just outside the pit. They can space there and keep hitting the monster while the enemy has to walk in.
- **The team inside the pit is itself in a chokepoint.** This is why a Rumble, Orianna or Amumu ult into the pit is the classic counter to an early start, and why you don't start Baron with five people inside the pit against an AoE comp.

**Which side or entrance to hold (inference):**
- Hold the entrance closest to the enemy's **most likely arrival route**. That route is set by where their side laner is and whether Teleport is up, and by which lane is crashed toward them.
- Keep one ward on the **second** entrance so a flank is seen.
- For dragon, that usually means the river side toward mid plus the entrance from the enemy bot jungle (tribrush and pixel side). For Baron, mirror it. In the All-Seeing form, add the tunnel.

**Fighting in fog (inference):**
- Without vision, neither team can see the other's numbers, so the team that acts on information it has (a ward seeing 3 enemies) beats the team guessing.
- Practical rules:
  1. Sweep, then sit in brush you have cleared.
  2. Don't step into unwarded brush with your carry. Face-check with the tank or with an ability.
  3. If the enemy holds vision and you don't, don't contest from the front. Either flank through the fog on a side they haven't warded, or trade.
